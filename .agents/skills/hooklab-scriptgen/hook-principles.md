# HookLab: Short-Form Video Principles (fallback reference)

General, cross-niche principles distilled from research on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. **This is a fallback.** Scriptgen's primary source is always the user's own proven library (`db_export.py`). Use these principles to supplement that, most useful when the library is thin, and prefer the user's own data wherever it conflicts. Rules tagged for a niche or platform apply only in that context; adapt everything to the ingested niche and what the user actually wants.

## Phase 1: Hook (first 3 seconds)

Universal:
- **No greeting intro.** Never open with "hey guys / welcome back". A greeting signals nothing interesting is coming and triggers a swipe.
- **Lead with the outcome.** Show or state the finished result or peak payoff first; the "after" creates an immediate "how?" that holds attention.
- **Specificity tax.** Use a concrete, non-rounded number ("$4,213", "19 days"), not vague terms; specifics read as credible.
- **Movement in the first frame.** Open on a pan, fast cut, or a subject entering/moving; movement interrupts the scroll reflex.
- **Call out the persona.** Name the intended viewer ("if you're a solo founder under 10k MRR") so the right person feels it's for them and stops.
- **High-key opening.** Brightly lit, higher exposure/saturation than later scenes; bright openings average markedly more engagement.
- **Eyes in the first second.** A face with eyes visible (no sunglasses) within ~1s drives human connection and retention.
- **Proof, Promise, Plan** (educational): early on, state credentials + what they'll learn + the structure ("I made $1M; I'll show you how; 4 steps").
- **Opening length.** Keep the spoken opening tight. Sources conflict (5-8 vs 10-14 words); stay short enough to process before a swipe decision.

Platform-specific:
- **Sub-1.5s instant hook** (TikTok / Shorts): land the curiosity gap or visual interrupt before 1.5s; the 3s mark is too slow here.
- **Three-layer alignment** (prioritised for Reels): spoken hook, visual action, and headline text all state the same specific subject; misalignment creates friction and churn.

## Phase 2: Retention (the middle)

Universal:
- **Pattern-interrupt rhythm.** A cut, zoom, camera move, text change, or B-roll switch every 2-4 seconds keeps the brain out of passive scroll mode.
- **UI safe zones.** Keep critical text and faces in the central 80%, clear of the top 10%, bottom 15%, and right-side icon column.
- **5th-grade vocabulary.** No jargon above a 5th-grade reading level; distracted viewers won't spend the processing energy.
- **Music under voice.** Music at roughly 8-15% while voiceover sits at 100%, so the voice stays dominant without losing energy.
- **Staccato first half.** Shorter, punchier sentences early (where attention is scarce) than in the back half.
- **"But" as pivot.** Transition setup to payoff with "but" / "however", not "and then", which reads as rambling.
- **Burn-in captions.** Large sans-serif, phrase-by-phrase (not full sentences); 60-85% of short-form is watched muted.

Platform-specific:
- **Blur topic transitions** (YouTube Shorts): start the next step's visual while still finishing the current sentence. Saying "moving on to step two" cues viewers they can leave.

## Phase 3: Payoff and CTA (the ending)

Universal:
- **Single-action CTA.** Ask for exactly one next step ("save this", or "comment GUIDE"), never a stack of follow/like/share/click.
- **CTA in the final 5-10s.** Too early interrupts the payoff; too late and they've swiped.
- **Match CTA to intent.** Educational → "save"; opinion → "comment"; relatable → "share".
- **Infinite-loop ending.** Make the final line or frame match the opening so the restart is seamless and rewatch metrics climb.

Platform-specific:
- **Conversion length 21-34s** (Reels / ads meant to drive leads or sales): room for hook, proof, and CTA without dragging.
- **Discovery length 7-15s** (Reels / TikTok for reach): ultra-short lifts completion rate, the main distribution signal.

## Adapting by niche

These are general defaults, not laws. When composing for a specific niche, weight toward what the user's own library proves works in that niche, and toward what the user says they want. Treat a universal principle as a strong prior and a niche/platform rule as conditional. If the library and a general principle disagree, the library wins, it is real evidence from this account.
